**Status:** Final consolidated research release

## Final deliverables

- Ten sequential Jupyter notebooks covering national health financing, state health budgets, health outcomes, hospital infrastructure, correlations, rural PHC staffing, adjusted workforce–IMR analysis, government beds per capita and state-level household insurance coverage and a separate hospitalisation expenditure analysis.
- Ten documented analysis-ready CSV datasets, with source and variable dictionaries.
- Thirteen reproducible SVG figures generated by the notebooks.
- A consolidated research report with research questions, data sources, methods, results, robustness checks, limitations and further research.
- NFHS-5 household insurance coverage with a documented state-scheme inventory comparison, including an A&N source discrepancy footnote.
- NSS 75th-round Table A17 state hospitalisation medical expenditure dataset, data dictionary, tenth notebook and thirteenth chart. The published table is gross medical expenditure, not net OOPE after reimbursement.
- A root README explaining the project, empirical strategy, findings, reproducibility and interpretation boundaries.
- Dataset, notebook and visualisation catalogues.
- A project validator and GitHub Actions workflow that checks datasets and notebook syntax, executes all nine notebooks and saves outputs/figures on successful pushes to `main`.

## Final empirical conclusions

1. National health financing shifted between FY2013–14 and FY2023–24: the OOPE share fell from 64.2% to 42.7%, while the government share rose from 28.6% to 43.5%.
2. Across 31 states/UTs, the 2024–25 Revised Estimate for Medical and Public Health and Family Welfare averaged 6.1% of aggregate expenditure (median 5.9%).
3. Of 34 matched states/UTs with a positive rural PHC doctor requirement, 10 were below the source-reported requirement; the median in-position-to-required ratio was 1.38.
4. Rural PHC staffing and 2023 infant mortality were negatively correlated in the 34-state/UT bivariate sample (Pearson r = −0.538; Spearman rho = −0.562).
5. In the 32-observation adjusted model, the preferred log staffing-ratio specification was negative (coefficient −7.619; HC3 p = 0.0345), while the full-sample linear-ratio coefficient was not statistically distinguishable from zero (−3.396; p = 0.2114). Restricted-sample and influence diagnostics are reported in the README, Notebook 07 and research report. The evidence is specification-sensitive and non-causal.
6. Government hospital beds per 1,000 projected population had a state/UT median of 0.949 and mean of 1.285, subject to the documented date gap and NHP source-table reconciliation issue.
7. NFHS-5 household coverage has near-zero correlation with per-capita NSDP (r=0.052, p=0.776; n=33). A non-exhaustive inventory documents state-specific schemes in 22 observations (mean coverage 50.8%; median 53.5%) versus 14 not documented as having one (mean 29.0%; median 25.4%). This is suggestive, not causal; the inventory is not exhaustive. Maharashtra is a counterexample to treating scheme presence as sufficient for high enrolment.
8. NSS 75th-round Table A17 provides state/UT average gross medical expenditure per hospitalisation case (excluding childbirth) for 2017–18. The all-hospital rural+urban values range from ₹4,754 to ₹54,870. These are not net OOPE after reimbursement.

## Reproducibility and quality controls

The current ten-notebook release is being revalidated, including dataset checks, notebook syntax, full execution and generated outputs. Reproduce locally from the repository root:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/validate_project.py
cd Notebooks
for notebook in *.ipynb; do
  jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute "$notebook" --inplace
done
```

## Research boundaries

This is a secondary-data, descriptive and correlational health-economics project. It does not estimate causal effects. National OOPE shares are not household catastrophic-expenditure rates. The state-level staffing ratio uses administrative post requirements, not population need. The adjusted model has a small cross-section and only two socioeconomic controls. Household catastrophic expenditure, rural–urban utilisation, a harmonised state panel and stronger causal identification remain outside the completed evidence base and are clearly identified as future research—not presented as completed findings.
